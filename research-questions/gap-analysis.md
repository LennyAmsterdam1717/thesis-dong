## Themes

- **T1 – UQ methods and their limits.** 2025–26 critical evaluations show uncertainty estimators break outside clean factual QA: near-random under ambiguity (tomov-2025-uq-ambiguity); output variability inflates and input ambiguity deflates estimates (goloburda-2026-uncertainty-sources); they ignore retrieved context in RAG (soudani-2025-ue-rag-axioms); semantic UQ depends on an off-the-shelf "same answer?" operator (hoche-2026-factual-equivalence); uncertainty predicts hallucination only conditionally (agnimo-2026-uq-vs-hallucination); accuracy is no proxy for uncertainty quality across 80 models (tao-2025-uq-80-models).
- **T2 – Overconfidence.** Systematic verbalized overconfidence (chhikara-2025-confidence-gap), with a causal internal circuit (zhao-2026-overconfidence-circuits), and competing biases in how LLMs update confidence (kumaran-2026-competing-biases).
- **T3 – People and LLM confidence.** Users over-rely on confident LLMs across languages (rathi-2025-overreliance-languages); gap between LLM confidence and users' perceived confidence (steyvers-2025-what-llms-know); learners absorb confident falsehoods (deepthika-s-2026-confident-llms-deference).
- **T4 – LLMs in finance.** Advice shifts with re-asked risk-tolerance answers (bakshi-2026-repeated-risk-tolerance); confident hallucinations in financial QA are visible in internal states but not in output confidence (wang-2026-confidently-wrong-finance); the value of a confidence score depends on the financial decision rule that consumes it (singh-2026-confidence-decision-operator); over-flagging in financial statement verification (panda-2026-finverbench).
- **T5 – Knowledge acquisition.** Temporal drift encoded orthogonally to correctness and uncertainty (elbadry-2026-temporal-drift); overconfidence follows knowledge popularity (ni-2025-popular-but-wrong); monofact rate, miscalibration and hallucination (miao-2026-monofacts-miscalibration); verbalized confidence traced to training data (xia-2026-tracing-verbalized-confidence); calibration-based knowledge probing (kissling-2026-knowledge-probing-calibration); finance: fine-tuning degrades numerical restraint (li-2026-financial-finetuning-hallucination), SFT vs CPT for institutional knowledge (ruan-2026-sft-cpt-financial-knowledge), fine-tuning vs RAG for regulatory facts (nyagar-2026-insurance-finetuning-rag); conformal guarantees under continual domain pretraining (zhou-2025-conformal-continual).
- **T6 – Epistemic diversity.** Seed wright-2025-epistemic-diversity (what and whose knowledge), kirsten-2026-epistemic-diversity (narrowness over valid answers), hodel-2025-knowledge-collapse (model diversity protects against collapse), and the bridge to T1: alignment-induced homogenization blinds sampling-based UQ (liu-2026-alignment-tax).

## Gaps

**G1 – Confidence on facts that change (time-sensitive financial knowledge).**
LLMs answer outdated facts confidently, and drift is encoded separately from correctness and uncertainty, so existing UQ cannot see it (elbadry-2026-temporal-drift). Models sharing stale snapshots converge on the same wrong answer in regulated domains while agreement scoring reports high confidence (martins-2026-convergent-hallucination; pilot, n = 10). Conformal guarantees need re-calibration after every domain update (zhou-2025-conformal-continual); popular wrong answers attract confidence (ni-2025-popular-but-wrong).
*Gap (corpus):* no study measures how confidence and calibration on **financial** facts (rates, limits, rules, prices) change with the age of the fact relative to the training cut-off.
**Scholar verdict: holds, narrowed.** li-2026-fincutbench measures how far a model's *financial* knowledge extends over calendar time, but by closed-book accuracy only (no model-confidence or calibration measure). Look-ahead-bias work in finance (DatedGPT 2026; Bradford, J. Accounting Research 2026; background) also targets accuracy/leakage. **Remaining gap:** confidence and calibration of LLMs on financial facts as a function of fact age vs cut-off, and whether confidence signals flag stale answers.

**G2 – Does how a fact was learned explain how sure the model is? (bridge between directions A and B)**
Popularity/co-occurrence predicts overconfidence (ni-2025-popular-but-wrong); hallucination is bounded by monofact rate minus miscalibration, and upweighting facts reshapes confidence (miao-2026-monofacts-miscalibration); verbalized confidence draws more on confidence-style than content training data (xia-2026-tracing-verbalized-confidence); intrinsic confidence in relational knowledge is overconfident (kissling-2026-knowledge-probing-calibration). In finance, fine-tuning degraded numerical restraint (li-2026-financial-finetuning-hallucination), CPT helped concepts but not exact values (ruan-2026-sft-cpt-financial-knowledge), and fine-tuning changed "how the model speaks, not what it knows" (nyagar-2026-insurance-finetuning-rag).
*Gap (corpus):* no controlled study varies the **acquisition route** of the same financial facts (pre-training frequency, fine-tuning, continued pre-training, in-context) and measures the resulting calibration of token-level and verbalized confidence.
**Scholar verdict: partly falls (general domain), holds narrowed.** wang-2025-prior-knowledge-calibration (ACL 2025) shows that fine-tuning data the model already knows induces overconfidence; Gekhman et al. 2024 (fine-tuning on new knowledge and hallucination) and Ovadia et al. 2024 (fine-tuning vs RAG, accuracy) are foundational background. **Remaining gap:** a *comparison of several acquisition routes* (pre-training frequency, SFT, CPT, RAG) for the *same* facts, measuring *both token-level and verbalized* confidence, on *financial* facts.

**G3 – Uncertainty for advice, not answers.**
UQ is validated on single-answer QA; it fails under ambiguity (tomov-2025-uq-ambiguity), confuses legitimate answer variety with uncertainty (goloburda-2026-uncertainty-sources), depends on what counts as "the same answer" (hoche-2026-factual-equivalence), and mishandles context (soudani-2025-ue-rag-axioms). Financial advice has no single correct answer, and LLM allocation advice already moves with irrelevant changes in a client's re-asked risk answer (bakshi-2026-repeated-risk-tolerance).
*Gap (corpus):* no uncertainty measure separates legitimate variation in advice from model uncertainty, and none is validated on financial advice.
**Scholar verdict: holds.** No uncertainty method for financial advice found; finance-advice studies on Scholar (e.g. SSRN 2026 papers) evaluate quality or bias, not uncertainty measurement.

**G4 – Homogeneous models make agreement a false confidence signal.**
Aligned models produce near-identical samples, leaving sampling-based UQ without signal (liu-2026-alignment-tax); LLM outputs are epistemically narrow (wright-2025-epistemic-diversity; kirsten-2026-epistemic-diversity); diversity across models protects against knowledge collapse (hodel-2025-knowledge-collapse); multi-model consensus can confidently converge on a stale fabrication (martins-2026-convergent-hallucination, pilot).
*Gap (corpus):* consistency- and consensus-based confidence is widely used, but its validity for financial questions under within- and cross-model homogeneity has not been tested. (Weaker than v0.1: the finance-specific convergence evidence could not be retrieved.)
**Scholar verdict: general part falls; finance-only remainder is thin.** hamidieh-2026-cross-model-disagreement (ICLR 2026) analyses exactly the homogeneous-failure case and adds cross-model disagreement; further 2026 preprints decompose wrong-consensus agreement. What remains is a finance replication — not strong enough for a stand-alone RQ.

**G5 – Expressed confidence in financial advice and how users respond.**
Verbalized overconfidence is systematic and mechanistically grounded (chhikara-2025-confidence-gap; zhao-2026-overconfidence-circuits; kumaran-2026-competing-biases); users follow confident LLMs (rathi-2025-overreliance-languages; steyvers-2025-what-llms-know; deepthika-s-2026-confident-llms-deference), but these user studies use trivia, science or general knowledge. In finance the evidence is model-side only (wang-2026-confidently-wrong-finance; singh-2026-confidence-decision-operator).
*Gap (corpus):* no study measures both the calibration of an LLM's expressed confidence in **financial advice** and the effect of that expression on retail users' decisions.
**Scholar verdict: absence claim withdrawn.** Spillner et al. (ACM CUI 2026; abstract only, full text not retrieved) study hedging matched to model confidence in financial decision-making with participants; an SSRN 2026 paper compares confidence and accuracy in AI financial advice. G5 is no longer used as a gap.

**G6 – Few finance benchmarks scored on calibration (supporting gap).**
Large UQ evaluations are general-domain (tao-2025-uq-80-models; agnimo-2026-uq-vs-hallucination); the nearest finance benchmark measures over-flagging rather than probability calibration (panda-2026-finverbench).
*Gap (corpus):* no time-stamped financial-knowledge benchmark scored on calibration. Supports G1/G2 as a data contribution rather than a separate RQ.

## Evidence strength (for the RQs)

Strong (peer-reviewed or large-scale): G2 (ni, miao, xia, kissling, li), G3 (tomov, goloburda, hoche, soudani), G5 model side (chhikara, zhao, kumaran, steyvers, rathi). Weak spots: G1 finance side rests on a pilot (martins); G4 finance side likewise; G2 finance strand includes two repository/preprint reports (ruan, nyagar).
