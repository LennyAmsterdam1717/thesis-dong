TI: MIRAGE: A Framework for Multi-hop Reasoning under Dynamic Knowledge Injection
AB: Multi-hop Question Answering (MQA) requires large language models (LLMs) to retrieve and synthesize multiple pieces of evidence into a coherent
    reasoning path, which is essential for complex tasks. However, prior work on knowledge updating for MQA has largely focused on simple fact edits,
    overlooking a more challenging and realistic setting---\emph{Knowledge Injection}---in which the model must simultaneously accommodate both
    conflicting updates and entirely novel information. Under this setting, a hop-level answer is often wrong simply because the model trusts the
    wrong knowledge source: it clings to outdated parametric memory when the injected knowledge conflicts with it (K2), and it cannot answer when the
    required fact is entirely new (K3). To investigate this, we introduce NEXUS, the first benchmark tailored for multi-hop reasoning under Knowledge
    Injection, which explicitly covers both conflicting (K2) and incremental (K3) knowledge. We further propose MIRAGE, an efficient, training-free
    framework whose core component, the Correlation-aware Dual-path Reasoner (CDR), resolves such per-hop source-trust errors via P(True)-based
    confidence routing, complemented by a lightweight template-guided corrector for residual structural deviations. Extensive experiments across four
    LLM families and two benchmarks demonstrate that MIRAGE consistently improves both final-answer accuracy and hop-level reasoning accuracy,
    establishing a new state of the art in this challenging setting.

    model (LLM)-based methods are mostly static, open-loop generations lacking dynamic adaptability. This article proposes a novel hybrid reasoning
    framework, a so-called LLM-guided rule dynamic optimization (LLM-RDO), by integrating the structural robustness captured by graph neural networks

    optimization. Finally, to achieve higher prediction accuracy, a joint prediction mechanism combining rule-based and embedding-based approaches is
    introduced. Experiments on 4 benchmark datasets demonstrate that LLM-RDO achieves superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art
    baseline methods.

    Language-Model Decision-Making

    retrieval-augmented generation, tool use, software development, autonomous agents, and collaborative problem-solving. Although these systems can
    generate candidate continuations and evaluate intermediate outputs, a decision that appears locally appropriate may lead to poor downstream
    reasoning, unnecessary computation, factual errors, tool failures, or incomplete task execution. This paper proposes Lookahead Large Language
    Models (L²M), a unified research framework for predicting the downstream consequences of candidate language-model actions before committing to
    them. The framework treats token generation, reasoning-step selection, context management, evidence acquisition, tool invocation, memory
    operations, model routing, and task termination as decision problems. Given a current state and a set of feasible actions, a lookahead controller
    estimates their future utility, uncertainty, risk, and computational cost, then selects an action according to an explicit decision objective. The
    framework separates state representation, candidate generation, outcome prediction, decision selection, execution, verification, and experimental
    recordkeeping. It can be implemented around an existing language model, incorporated into an inference-time controller, or adapted to training and
    agent orchestration. A modular Research Slot Architecture is introduced to support independent implementation and evaluation. To facilitate
    systematic research development, this paper presents 300 candidate research directions across fifteen LLM-specific research categories, including
    token-level generation, reasoning, context processing, retrieval, tool use, memory, multi-agent systems, code intelligence, mathematics, factual
    reliability, multilingual behavior, personalization, inference efficiency, evaluation, and model training. The catalogue is intended as a
    structured research roadmap rather than a claim that every direction is novel or effective. The central hypothesis is that explicit prediction of
    downstream consequences can improve LLM decisions when candidate actions have materially different future effects and the expected benefit
    justifies the cost of prediction. This hypothesis requires controlled comparisons against fixed, greedy, heuristic, and learned non-lookahead
    baselines.

TI: Soft-Prompt Personal Memory: Pluggable Factual Knowledge Injection via Prefix Tuning
AB: We propose Soft-Prompt Personal Memory (SPPM), a lightweight method for injecting arbitrary factual knowledge into frozen large language models
