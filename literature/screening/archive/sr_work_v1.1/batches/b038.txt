TI: AXIOM: Zero-Retrieval Knowledge Injection for Offline Medical LLMs via Hyperdimensional Computing
AB: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become the dominant paradigm for grounding Large Language Models (LLMs) in external knowledge. However,
    standard RAG incurs significant latency from vector-database searches and struggles to operate in fully offline, resource-constrained environments
    such as rural clinics or field hospitals. We introduce AXIOM (Axiomatic Hyperdimensional Injection for Offline Medical LLMs), a neurosymbolic
    architecture that replaces the retrieve-then-generate pipeline with three tightly integrated components: A Relational Contextual Distiller that
    compresses a multi-gigabyte medical corpus into a hierarchy of 10,000-dimensional Hyperdimensional Computing (HDC) "Axiom Maps" using holographic
    binding and superposition. A Zero-Retrieval Latent Priming mechanism that injects the Axiom Map directly into the KV-cache of a quantised Small
    Language Model (SLM), eliminating the retrieval step entirely. A Neurosymbolic Safety Governor that performs real-time token-level hallucination
    detection by comparing generated tokens against the Axiom Map in HDC space. Evaluated on both a controlled synthetic knowledge graph (F1=0.995,
    99% retrieval accuracy) and real-world medical datasets (PubMedQA and MedQA at 10,000 facts extracted via NER), AXIOM achieves a 565x compression
    ratio over FAISS in bipolar mode, >98% hallucination catch rates on noisy clinical data (F1 > 0.97 at both 5K and 10K scales), and a 29.7x query
    speedup—all while running entirely offline on commodity hardware. Keywords: Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG, Hyperdimensional Computing,
    Offline AI, Medical LLMs, Edge Computing, Neurosymbolic AI

TI: Process-aware programming assessment through temporal knowledge graph reasoning and LLM verification
AB: Automated programming assessment systems predominantly evaluate final code submissions while ignoring the problem-solving processes that reveal
    genuine learning, and large language model (LLM)-based feedback systems suffer from hallucinations due to the absence of structured domain
    knowledge grounding. This paper proposes a Process-AwareEvaluation Framework that is based on a combination of temporal knowledge graph reasoning
    with KG-augmented LLM verification, which can be used for the overall assessment of programming assignments. The proposed framework includes four
    modules that are tightly coupled and connected: Process-Aware Temporal Knowledge Graphs(PA-TKG), which models the evolution of code, concept
    coverage, and problem-solving activities as a timestamped heterogeneous graph structure; Composition-based Graph Convolutional Networks (CompGCN),
    which learn embeddings of relation-aware representations across different edge types; Contrastive Process Pattern Learning(CPPL)with a
    dual-encoder architecture that compares static code structure to dynamic development patterns to detect anomalous behavior; and KG-Guided
    Chain-of-Thought Verification (KG-CoTV), which constrains LLM reasoning against valid learning paths derived from a knowledge graph. The
    experiments conducted using two publicly available datasets demonstrated that the proposed framework achieved a process evaluation score of 0.912,
    indicating a 26.1 percent increase compared to the best performing baseline, as well as 62.8 percent higher anomaly detection accuracy and an 83.3
    percent decrease in feedback hallucination rates. Ablation studies were also performed to verify the contributions of each module toward the
    overall framework performance, where it was found that the Process-Aware Temporal Knowledge Graphs (PA-TKG)provided the most significant
    representational basis. The core non-LLM pipeline (graph construction, embedding, anomaly detection, and constraint checking)takes less than 500
    ms to process submissions; when combined with LLM inference for feedback generation, the total end-to-end latency averages 3.26 s on an NVIDIA
    A100 GPU, making the proposed framework suitable for near-real-time deployment in educational environments.

TI: Active Topological Learning (ATL): A Geometric Framework for Targeted Knowledge Injection in Small Language Models
AB: We propose Active Topological Learning (ATL), a geometric methodology for surgically inserting targeted conceptual knowledge into the embedding
    space of pre-trained Small Language Models (SLMs). Current fine-tuning paradigms require large datasets, significant compute, and risk degrading
    existing model knowledge. ATL addresses this by operating at the geometric level: detecting conceptual voids using the Quality Signal metric,
    measuring existing manifold structure via Angular Pre-Survey, designing a geometrically derived Micro-Corpus, and training exclusively along
    identified load-bearing dimensions using anisotropic gradient masking. We demonstrate that a synthetic concept with zero pre-training bias can be
    teleported from a random sub-word cluster to a precisely localized topological neighbourhood in the quantum computing manifold in under 40 seconds
    on consumer laptop hardware, without causing catastrophic forgetting in unrelated semantic regions. Subsequent natural language semantic retrieval
    confirms the inserted concept is discoverable by naive queries without prior knowledge of its existence. We identify a Semantic Singularity upper
    bound on training intensity and propose the concept of a minimum propagation radius R for bridge resolution. We suggest these findings raise open
    questions about just-in-time knowledge injection and the feasibility of on-demand model specialisation in resource-constrained deployments,
    ultimately proposing that the SLM serves best as a fluid topological substrate paving the way for deterministic external graph routing.

TI: Effective Reinforcement Learning for Remote Sensing Vision-Language Models via Supervised Knowledge Injection
AB: While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in general-domain visual understanding, their performance in remote
    sensing tasks remains limited due to domain discrepancies and the scarcity of remote sensing data in typical vision-language pretraining corpora.
    Recent advances show that reinforcement learning (RL) can enhance VLMs’ reasoning capabilities by guiding models to perform intermediate “thought”
    steps before answering. However, applying RL directly to remote sensing scenarios is often ineffective, as VLMs lack fundamental domain knowledge
    to begin with. To address this, we propose a novel paradigm that first equips VLMs with remote sensing knowledge in remote sensing visual
    grounding task through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a curated dataset of CoT samples specific to remote sensing. We then conduct RL to further

    Furthermore, evaluations on the out-of-domain dataset demon-strate that our method fosters better generalization in remote sensing reasoning. Our
    approach offers a promising direction for adapting general-purpose VLMs to domain-specific visual understanding tasks.

TI: Revolutionizing safety training: enhancing safety knowledge acquisition, retention, and user experience with adaptive VR-LLM integration
AB: Safety training is essential for preventing industrial accidents. The integration of Virtual Reality (VR) and Large Language Models (LLMs) holds
    transformative potential for safety training, as each technology has critical limitations when used alone. While VR provides realistic, risk-free
    simulations, it lacks adaptability to individual learning needs. Conversely, LLMs offer personalized feedback but lack contextual immersion.
    Guided by adaptive learning theory, this study proposes a framework which integrating VR and LLMs for safety training, namely AIVR. AIVR consists
    three core components: an AI tutoring system, that leveraging LLMs for personalized content generation; an adaptive learning system that
    continuously assesses learner performance and adjusts training content to address individual knowledge gaps; a VR system that contributes adaptive
    navigation support by visually emphasizing weak points, highlighting relevant areas in the immersive environment, and restructuring interaction
    paths in response to learner progress.This study then compared AIVR with traditional VR and slide-based instruction in an experiment assessing
    knowledge acquisition, retention, and user experience. Results demonstrated that AIVR significantly improved knowledge acquisition and retention,
    and outperformed the other methods in self-efficacy and technology acceptance. The findings also indicated that AIVR's personalization enhanced
    engagement and reduced anxiety. These results highlight the effectiveness of applying adaptive learning theory to combine immersive and
    intelligent technologies for enhancing safety training.

TI: Anticipating Object Interactions Via Aggregation and Distillation of Spatio-Temporal Knowledge From Vision Language Models
AB: The increasing prevalence of wearable cameras has driven the development of egocentric (first-person) systems that assist human activities
    proactively by anticipating imminent interactions. A central challenge in this domain is active object interaction anticipation from first-person
    video-predicting what interaction will occur, when it will happen, and where it will take place. This involves forecasting (1) what interaction
    category (verb-noun pair), (2) when (time-to-interaction), and (3) where (the active object's location, bounding box in the last observed frame).
    However, existing approaches rely on limited prior knowledge about active objects and their state changes, and they struggle to (1) predict
    diverse state-change interactions, (2) handle temporal uncertainty of changes, and (3) localize the active object accurately in the presence of
    spatial distractors. To address these problems, we propose ST-KAD. It consists of a Spatial-Temporal Knowledge Aggregator that integrates rich
    commonsense priors to enhance what-when-where interaction anticipation and guides the model's attention toward informative cues, and a
    Teacher-Student Distillation framework that enables efficient inference without access to oracle inputs by transferring knowledge from an
    oracle-informed teacher model to a query-based student decoder. On two egocentric anticipation benchmarks (Ego4D-STA, EPIC-Kitchens-STA), ST-KAD
    sets a new state of the art, demonstrating accurate what-when-where prediction of future interactions. Moreover, results on four active object
    detection benchmarks (Ego4D-AOD, EPIC-Kitchens-AOD, MECCANO, 100DOH) further confirm that our prior-informed aggregation and teacher-student
    distillation generalize beyond anticipation to spatial localization, validating the generality of the design.

TI: Refusal-Aware Instruction Tuning for Hallucination Mitigation near Uncertain Knowledge Boundaries
AB: The rapid deployment of large language models has exposed significant vulnerabilities in their reliability, particularly concerning their
    propensity to hallucinate when confronted with queries that exceed their internalized knowledge capacities. This paper investigates the critical
    challenge of mitigating hallucinations near uncertain knowledge boundaries through a novel paradigm termed refusal-aware instruction tuning.
    Conventional instruction tuning often inadvertently encourages models to generate responses even when uncertain, prioritizing fluency and
    helpfulness over factual accuracy. To counteract this phenomenon, we propose a systematic framework that integrates uncertainty quantification
    directly into the instruction tuning pipeline, teaching models to explicitly refuse to answer when a query falls outside their reliable knowledge
    distribution. By formalizing the concept of uncertain knowledge boundaries, we develop a specialized dataset curation strategy that identifies
    low-confidence regions and pairs them with appropriate refusal responses. Our methodology is subsequently evaluated through extensive empirical
    analysis, demonstrating that refusal-aware instruction tuning substantially reduces hallucination rates without compromising the utility of the
    model on well-known facts. The findings suggest that aligning models with their own epistemic limitations is a fundamental requirement for
    deploying trustworthy artificial intelligence systems in high-stakes domains.

TI: Enhanced temporal knowledge graph reasoning through TKG-LPEHD: LLM-driven dynamic fusion of graph and rule-based methods
AB: Temporal Knowledge Graphs are essential for understanding dynamic, time-evolving real-world scenarios. However, the effective modeling of temporal
    validity during reasoning remains a critical and often overlooked challenge. Conventional methods relying on static representations or predefined
    temporal windows struggle to capture the context-dependent nature of rule applicability over time, leading to inaccurate predictions and a
    constrained understanding of evolving relationships. To address these issues, this study proposes TKG-LPEHD, a novel framework that deeply
    integrates rule-based reasoning, graph-based reasoning and large language models to dynamically model the temporal validity of rules. A key
    feature is the LLM-guided mechanism for dynamic rule extraction and dynamic weight fusion reasoning. The framework employs an LLM-guided
    three-dimensional relational path sampling strategy with LLM assisted validity assessment, and fuses rule-based and graph-based reasoning through
    dynamic weights to ensure both high quality and temporal relevance of inference rules. The design further optimizes Neo4j storage architecture,
    temporal SPARQL query engineering and systematically tuned prompt engineering to facilitate real-world deployment. Evaluations on public
    benchmarks and a confidential Chinese temporal knowledge dataset demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art
    methods and achieves outstanding performance on long-range temporal reasoning tasks. This research not only validates the paradigm of explicitly
    incorporating temporal validity as a core reasoning dimension but also provides a more explainable and time-aware solution for high-stakes
    applications including financial forecasting and geopolitical analysis.

AB: Purpose This study aims to investigate the effects of different types of chatbots on knowledge learning performance and continuous usage intention
    by designing thought-guided and affective thought-guided chatbots. Drawing on coping theory, the study further seeks to uncover the underlying
    mechanism, focusing on perceptions of three nuanced aspects of transparency—social, expressive and diagnostic—and cognitive effort in
    human–artificial intelligence interactions. Design/methodology/approach Two experiments were conducted to investigate the impacts. A

    lowest continuous usage intention. The mechanism test reveals that, compared to the search engine, the affective thought-guided chatbot increases
    users’ perceptions of all three aspects of transparency and cognitive effort. Whereas the thought-guided chatbot only increases perceived
    expressive transparency and cognitive effort. Furthermore, perceived expressive transparency and perceived diagnostic transparency improve
    knowledge learning performance, perceived social transparency and perceived diagnostic transparency promote continuous usage intention and
    cognitive effort enhances knowledge learning performance but weakens continuous usage intention. Originality/value This study contributes to the
    literature by concretizing coping theory in the chatbot context and dissecting interaction transparency into three nuanced aspects from a more
    detailed perspective. It addresses the ongoing debate over whether chatbots will replace search engines. It further introduces two innovative
    types of chatbots and presents a comprehensive framework to reveal the underlying mechanism.

TI: A Provable Optimization Framework for Knowledge Forgetting in Transformer Models
AB: As Transformer-based language models increasingly serve as repositories of factual knowledge, the ability to selectively forget specific
    information becomes crucial for correcting outdated facts, mitigating harmful content, or adhering to privacy regulations. However, current model
    editing methods either lack formal guarantees or incur undesirable interference with unrelated knowledge. In this work, we propose Provable
    Knowledge Forgetting (PKF), a rigorous framework that enables targeted suppression of factual information while preserving the model’s overall
    functionality. PKF formulates forgetting as a constrained optimization problem bounded by a Semantic Forgetting Bound, which quantifies the safe
    deviation from the model’s original behavior using Fisher Information. To make this tractable, we introduce a low-rank Fisher projection mechanism
    that ensures localized edits and a regularization term that minimizes semantic drift. We validate PKF on three benchmark datasets CounterFact,
    zsRE, and TriviaQA using five evaluation metrics: Forget@1, AvgKL, Knowledge Interference Score (KIS), KISnorm, and Neighborhood Consistency Rate
    (NCR). Our experiments show that PKF outperforms strong baselines such as ROME, MEND, and KE, achieving over 94% forgetting success with
    significantly lower interference. This work offers a scalable, theoretically grounded solution for controllable forgetting in language models,
    with implications for safety, compliance, and model lifespan management.
