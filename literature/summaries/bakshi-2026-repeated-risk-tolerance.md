# Same Person, Different Advice: Auditing LLM Financial Advice with Repeated Risk-Tolerance Responses

**Soovadeep Bakshi et al. (2026)** · arXiv preprint · preprint · https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23060
_Paraphrased note by Claude (single reviewer) from the abstract, introduction and discussion/limitations; full text checked 2026-11-20._

- **Question:** How stable is LLM financial advice when a client's risk answer changes the way real people's answers do?
- **Method:** Seven LLMs, 800 real household profiles where only the stated risk answer changes; benchmark from panel and lab retest data.
- **Main finding:** Recommended stock share moves by large amounts with plausible re-test changes in the risk answer; most models exceed a reliability-based benchmark.
- **Limitations:** No ground truth for the right advice; measures consistency with each model's own rule, not correctness.
- **Relevance for this thesis:** G3 (finance, key): advice variability is measurable - but not yet as model uncertainty.
