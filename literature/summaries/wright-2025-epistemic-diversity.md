# What and Whose Knowledge? Measuring Epistemic Diversity in Large Language Models

**Dustin Wright et al. (2025)** · EMNLP 2026 (accepted; seed paper 1) · peer-reviewed · https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04226
_Paraphrased note by Claude (single reviewer) from the abstract, introduction and discussion/limitations; full text checked 2026-11-20._

- **Question:** How diverse are the real-world claims LLMs present, and whose knowledge is represented? (seed paper)
- **Method:** 27 LLMs, 155 topics, 1.7M responses, 70M claims; diversity over time and across countries; comparison with web search.
- **Main finding:** Epistemic diversity has improved over model generations, but all models remain less diverse than a traditional web search, and salient gaps remain in whose (country) knowledge is represented.
- **Limitations:** See paper limitations (topic and country sampling).
- **Relevance for this thesis:** G4/T6: seed; framing for whose knowledge and homogeneity. EMNLP 2026.
