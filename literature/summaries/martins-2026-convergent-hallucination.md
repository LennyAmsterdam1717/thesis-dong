# Convergent Hallucination in Multi-LLM Consensus: A Two-Layer Guard with Empirical Evidence in UK/EU Regulated Domains

**Jaqueline Martins et al. (2026)** · repository draft (Zenodo) · preprint · https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21314594
_Paraphrased note by Claude (single reviewer) from the abstract, introduction and discussion/limitations; full text checked 2026-11-20._

- **Question:** Tests whether agreement between several LLMs reduces fabrication in regulated advice domains.
- **Method:** Pilot (n = 10 questions) in UK/EU regulated domains with two providers and a rule-based judge; proposes a two-layer guard.
- **Main finding:** Models sharing stale training snapshots converged on the same fabricated answer, and semantic-agreement scoring reported high confidence in it.
- **Limitations:** Explicitly a pilot: n = 10, two providers, rule-based judge, single language; author calls it 'signal, not proof'. Workshop draft on Zenodo.
- **Relevance for this thesis:** G1/G4: illustrative only - consensus as false confidence under stale knowledge. Do not lean on it alone.
