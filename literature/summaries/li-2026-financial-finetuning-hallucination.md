# When Financial Fine-tuning Fails: A Three-Level Detectability Analysis of Numerical Hallucination in Domain-Adapted Language Models

**Xiaodong Li et al. (2026)** · Neurocomputing (2026) · peer-reviewed · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2026.135011 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04806
_Paraphrased note by Claude (single reviewer) from the abstract, introduction and discussion/limitations; full text checked 2026-11-20._

- **Question:** Is numerical hallucination in financial summarisation a reasoning failure?
- **Method:** Controlled comparison of a base model and two fine-tuned variants; three levels of detectability of numerical hallucination.
- **Main finding:** Fine-tuning on financial data destroyed 'numerical restraint': hallucination rose from near zero (base) to 82.5-90.8% (fine-tuned) at all detectability levels.
- **Limitations:** Small-to-mid model scale; larger models may differ (authors' discussion).
- **Relevance for this thesis:** G2 (finance, key): the acquisition route (domain fine-tuning) changes how the model commits to numbers. Neurocomputing.
